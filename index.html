    02 · How the score is made

    Three encoders, one ordinal head

    The photo, the sentence and the pin are each weak on their own. A hanging limb over a footpath and a hanging limb over a field are the same photograph and a different emergency — only the pin knows the difference.

    STEP 1

    Intake & gating

    Before any model runs, cheap checks that stop wasted work.

    • EXIF GPS vs. parcel layer
    • Municipal vs. private ownership
    • Duplicate cluster: geohash-7 + image-embedding cosine > 0.92
    STEP 2

    Vision branch

    Detector finds defects and their targets; classifier rates severity.

    • 9 defect classes + lean angle
    • Deadwood fraction of crown
    • Pooled 768-d embedding
    STEP 3

    Text & geo branch

    The resident's words give urgency and target; the pin gives consequence.

    • Sentence embedding + urgency head
    • Joins the street-tree inventory
    • ~40 exposure & weather features
    STEP 4

    Fusion & ordinal head

    P1–P4 is an ordered scale, so the head predicts thresholds, not classes.

    • Concatenated 1,192-d vector
    • Cumulative-link (CORN) head
    • Under-triage costs 8× over-triage
    STEP 5

    Calibrate & route

    A probability you can act on, then a decision rule — not an argmax.

    • Isotonic calibration per band
    • Abstain band → arborist
    • Threshold shifts in storm mode
    03 · Model card

    What to train, and with what settings

    Starting point sized for a municipal dataset — roughly 30k historical requests, of which maybe 2–3k get arborist-grade labels. That budget, not the leaderboard, picks the architecture.

    Vision branch

    Two heads on the resident's photo: what is wrong (detection) and how badly (severity). Fine-tuned from ImageNet rather than trained from scratch — there will never be enough tree photos to do otherwise.

    ParameterValue
    DetectorYOLOv8-s, imgsz 640, conf 0.25, NMS IoU 0.50
    Classeshanging limb, fresh fracture, codominant/included bark, cavity & decay, conk, root-plate lift, sidewalk heave, deadwood, storm snap
    Severity netConvNeXt-Tiny, 384×384, pooled 768-d
    OptimizerAdamW, head 3e-4 / backbone 3e-5, cosine, 5% warmup
    Schedulebatch 32, 30 epochs, wd 0.05, label smoothing 0.05, EMA 0.999
    Augmentflip, RandomResizedCrop 0.7–1.0, colour jitter 0.3, motion blur, rain/fog overlay
    WhyCitizen photos are handheld, wet, backlit and often taken at night. The weather augmentations matter more than the backbone choice.

    Text branch

    Short, misspelled, emotional. The job is not sentiment — it is extracting target ("over my kid's bus stop") and tempo ("since the storm", "about to come down").

    ParameterValue
    EncoderDistilRoBERTa / MiniLM-L6, 384-d, max_len 256
    Fine-tunelr 2e-5, batch 16, 4 epochs, dropout 0.1
    Aux headsurgency (ordinal 0–3), target type (multi-label, 7 tags)
    Cold startLLM few-shot extraction to structured JSON, then distil into the encoder once ~1k labels exist
    Watch forUrgency words are a proxy for how assertive the caller is. Cap the text branch's contribution so a calm report of a falling tree still outranks an angry one about a leaf drop.

    Geo & tabular branch

    The pin is the highest-value signal and the cheapest. It turns a hazard into a consequence by naming what stands underneath.

    Feature groupExamples
    Target exposuredistance to sidewalk, road class, transit stop, school/playground, building footprint, parking
    Asset joinspecies, DBH, planting year, last inspection, prior work orders within 50 m
    Sitesoil volume, slope, coastal exposure, overhead line buffer, ward
    Weather72 h forecast gust, 48 h rainfall, saturation index, freeze–thaw count
    ModelLightGBM — leaves 31, depth 6, lr 0.05, 800 rounds w/ early stop 50, min_data_in_leaf 40, feature_fraction 0.8, bagging 0.8, L2 1.0
    Constraintsmonotone: defect severity ↑, footfall ↑, distance-to-target ↓

    Fusion, loss & evaluation

    Recommended first build: freeze the encoders, concatenate, and let gradient boosting do the fusion. It is the most data-efficient option and it explains itself — both of which matter more here than a point of accuracy.

    ParameterValue
    Fusion vector768 (image) + 384 (text) + 40 (tabular) = 1,192-d
    Head A (start)LightGBM ordinal — 3 cumulative binary targets P≥2, P≥3, P≥4
    Head B (later)MLP 1192→256→128, GELU, dropout 0.3, CORN loss, end-to-end
    Class weightsinverse frequency; cost matrix penalising under-triage 8×
    Calibrationisotonic regression on a held-out 15%, checked by ECE
    Primary metricquadratic weighted kappa (ordinal agreement)
    Safety metricP1 recall at fixed review capacity; under-triage rate < 2%
    Splitsgroup by tree ID, split by time — train pre-storm, test post-storm
    04 · Before it goes live

    The parts that decide whether anyone trusts it

    Labels come from work orders

    Bootstrap from what crews actually did and how fast — then have arborists re-label a stratified sample of ~2,500 and measure inter-rater agreement first. If two arborists disagree 30% of the time, that is your accuracy ceiling.

    Reporting is not risk

    Affluent streets call 311 more. Audit P1 recall per ward and per income decile; a tree's priority must never depend on how many people complained about it.

    Storm mode

    After a Fiona-scale event, volume jumps 40× and the decision rule must change with it: shift the auto-dispatch threshold, widen the abstain band, and re-rank by target exposure rather than defect severity.

    Overrides are the product

    Log every arborist correction with the image and features. Active-learning on low-confidence and high-disagreement cases is worth more than another 10k unlabelled photos.

    Drift

    Leaf-on and leaf-off crowns look nothing alike. Monitor embedding drift by month, and retrain seasonally rather than annually.

    It recommends; it never closes

    The model produces a priority and a reason. A person dispatches. That line is what makes the audit trail defensible when a limb comes down anyway.
